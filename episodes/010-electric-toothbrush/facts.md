# Facts — What's inside an electric toothbrush? (ep. 10)

| Claim | Source |
|---|---|
| Rechargeable toothbrushes charge by induction: a coil in the base, a coil in the handle, no physical/metal contact (contacts would corrode in a wet bathroom) | https://www.thenakedscientists.com/node/179836 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_toothbrush ("newer toothbrushes use inductive charging") |
| Alternating current in the base coil makes a changing magnetic field; "although electricity will not travel through the plastic, magnetism will"; it induces current in the handle coil | https://www.thenakedscientists.com/node/179836 |
| Sealed handle, nothing exposed, so moisture can't reach live parts | https://www.thenakedscientists.com/node/179836 |
| Inside a sonic brush: Li-ion battery, circuit board with microcontroller, a drive coil and a charging coil | https://www.righto.com/2016/09/sonicare-toothbrush-teardown.html |
| Sonic drive: pulsing a coil in alternating directions moves a magnet back and forth, limited to a few degrees, so it vibrates rather than spinning like a motor | https://www.righto.com/2016/09/sonicare-toothbrush-teardown.html |
| "Up to 31,000 brush strokes per minute" (sonic brush spec) | https://www.documents.philips.com/assets/20220728/f7f32106977a42b6a407aee00135eb85.pdf ; righto.com teardown |
| Timer: many brushes buzz/stop after two minutes (sometimes every 30 s) | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_toothbrush |
| Broxodent, invented by Philippe-Guy Woog in Switzerland in 1954, "plugged into a standard wall outlet and ran on line voltage" (a 1937 Motodent patent is earlier, so the script says "an early" electric toothbrush, not "the first") | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_toothbrush |
| Safety: Li-ion cell inside, do not puncture | general Li-ion safety |
